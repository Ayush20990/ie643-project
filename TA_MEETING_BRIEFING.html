Baby dictionary — say these words only after you can explain them

If the TA uses a jargon word, translate it to this. All of these appear in our project.

WordPlain meaning
Time seriesNumbers that come in order over time (hourly weather, daily stock, hourly transformer).
MultivariateSeveral numbers at once. Weather is 7 variables (temp, humidity, pressure…). Not just temperature alone.
Look-back L / horizon HL = how much past we show the model. H = how far ahead we ask it to guess. Climate/energy: 168 hours in, 24 hours out. Finance: 60 days in, 5 days out.
WindowOne cut of the series: those L past rows, plus the H future rows we want to guess.
DomainA place / setting. Domain 1 = source (old place). Domain 2 = target (new place).
Label / answerThe true future. In Domain 1 we have it. In Domain 2, for training, we pretend we do not.
Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA)Teach the old model the new place using only the new place’s past, never its future.
Domain shiftThe two places do not behave the same. Delhi is hotter; pharma stocks move differently from IT.
AlignmentMake the model’s internal notes (the 128-number summary) look similar in both places, so the “guess tomorrow” head can work in both.
Latent / z / summaryThose 128 numbers the encoder squeezes the past into. This is what we align.
CheckpointA saved snapshot of the weights. “Picking a checkpoint” = choosing which snapshot to keep as the final model.
SeedA starting number for all the randomness (weight start, batch order). Seed 0, seed 1, seed 2 are three independent reruns of the same recipe. If a method is good, it should win on more than one seed. We report the mean and the spread (std) over 3 seeds. CARE-DA v2 so far has only seed 0 — that is why we call it a probe, not a locked result.
EpochOne full pass through the training data. Source model: 20 epochs. Adaptation: 10 epochs. Oracle (with labels): 15 epochs.
BatchA small group of windows updated together (256 from Domain 1 + 256 from Domain 2 in one adaptation step).
Learning rateHow big a step the weights take. Source 1e-3 (bigger). Adaptation 3e-4 (smaller, so we do not smash the old model).
BaselineA simple or classic method we compare against, so we can say “did we beat the obvious thing?”
AblationTake our method and turn one piece off. If turning a piece off makes things worse, that piece was useful. If turning it off makes things better, that piece was hurting.
SSL / pretext / homeworkA fake task we can score without answers (hide-and-rebuild the past). We hoped it would help. It did not.
Negative transferAdaptation made Domain 2 worse than doing nothing. DANN and CORAL-alone did this.
OracleA cheat we are not allowed to use in the real product: start from Domain 1, then fine-tune with Domain 2 answers. It is the ceiling: “how well could we do if someone labelled the new place?”
Target-onlyAnother cheat: train a new model from scratch using only Domain 2 answers. Also not UDA. Used only as a reference.
Source-onlyDo nothing. Take the Domain 1 model and run it on Domain 2. This is the floor we must beat.
Seasonal naiveEven simpler: “tomorrow = same time yesterday / last season.” No learning. If we cannot beat this, the model is useless.

The car story — one car, many ways of driving it

LiteTCN is the car. It is the only neural network we built. Same engine, same seats, same 128-number “dashboard summary” of the last week. We did not build a DANN network, an MMD network, and a CARE network. We built one car, then tried different driving lessons after it had already learned Shimla.

First we teach the car Shimla for 20 epochs. That parked car is the source model. Then we take a copy of that parked car and give it a new lesson on Delhi. Each name in the table is a different lesson. After the lesson we ask: “how wrong is Delhi tomorrow?”

We repeat every lesson 3 times (seeds 0, 1, 2) except CARE-DA v2, which we only did once so far.

Lesson 0 — not even a car

Seasonal naive. No LiteTCN. “Tomorrow at 3 pm = yesterday at 3 pm.” We keep this so we can say the car is at least smarter than copy-paste. MASE uses this as the divisor.

Lesson 1 — park the car and do nothing

Source-only. Drive the Shimla-trained LiteTCN straight into Delhi. No extra lesson. This is “what happens if we skip the whole project.” Every −3.6% later means “this lesson beat source-only by 3.6%.”

Lessons 2–4 — classic driving schools (one trick each)

These three start from the same parked Shimla car. They only see Delhi’s past, never Delhi’s tomorrow.

DANN (adversarial). We hire a cop who looks at the car’s dashboard summary and tries to guess: “are you in Shimla or Delhi?” We train the car to make the cop fail. Idea: if the cop cannot tell the two cities apart, the dashboard must look the same, so the “guess tomorrow” part might work in Delhi. In our runs the cop fight went badly. The dashboard became messy and Delhi forecasts got worse, especially energy (+26%).

Deep CORAL. Ignore the cop. Only make the correlations on the dashboard match: if temperature and humidity move together in Shimla, make them move together the same way in Delhi. That alone was not enough. Worse than doing nothing on all three datasets.

MMD. Look at a bag of Shimla dashboard-summaries and a bag of Delhi ones. MMD is a ruler that says “how different are these two bags?” We push that number down. No cop, no extra homework. This simple lesson was the best 3-seed result on climate (MSE 0.254 → 0.234, temp 1.77 °C → 1.39 °C).

Lesson 5 — our own driving school (CARE-DA v1)

Still the same car. We combine four rules in one lesson, because the theory said we needed all of them:

  1. Keep passing the Shimla test — still guess Shimla’s tomorrow, so we do not forget the old city.
  2. Match the dashboards — MMD + CORAL together (the two alignment tricks).
  3. Homework with no answer sheet — hide 30% of Delhi’s past and ask the car to rebuild it. We thought this would keep the dashboard useful. This is SSL.
  4. Spring / anchor — do not wander too far from the parked Shimla weights. Like a bungee so the car cannot throw away everything it knew.

This is the method we designed and reported as ours. It helps (climate MSE 0.245, energy 0.167) but it is not the best, because rule 3 (homework) pulled the car toward “rebuild the past” instead of “guess the future.”

Lessons 6–8 — take our school and pull one rule out (ablations)

An ablation is not a new car. It is the same CARE lesson with one rule unplugged, so we can see which rule mattered.

CARE − align. Unplug MMD and CORAL. Keep homework + spring. Crashes. So matching dashboards was the useful part.

CARE − SSL. Unplug the hide-and-rebuild homework. Keep alignment + spring. This is the best 3-seed no-label driver overall. Climate almost ties MMD. Energy even beats the labelled oracle.

CARE − anchor. Unplug the spring. Fine on climate, almost useless on energy. So the spring matters when the two cities are very different.

Lesson 9 — new school, same car (CARE-DA v2)

We heard the ablation: homework was the bad rule. So v2 still uses LiteTCN, still starts from the Shimla car, still uses no Delhi answers, but:

  • throws away hide-and-rebuild
  • adds a homework that is closer to forecasting (guess a nearer future from Delhi’s past)
  • matches dashboards at two scales, and also matches frequency (how “wiggly” the series is)

We only drove this lesson once (seed 0). It looks better. The app’s Domain-2 button uses this car. We have not proven it over 3 seeds yet.

Lessons 10–11 — cheating, only so we know the ceiling

These use Delhi’s tomorrow. The brief does not allow this in the product. We still ran them so we can say “how much is left on the table?”

Target-only. Buy a brand-new LiteTCN. Teach it only Delhi, with answers. Sometimes worse than adapting the old car (energy), which means Shimla knowledge was useful.

Fine-tune oracle. Take the Shimla car and keep driving it on Delhi with the answer sheet. Best possible if labels existed. Climate can still improve a bit more (MSE 0.232). Finance barely moves (0.548 vs source 0.561). The oracle also forgets Shimla (climate Domain-1 error +14%). Our no-label lessons do not forget.

Say this to the TA: “LiteTCN is the model. DANN, MMD, CORAL, CARE are not other models. They are different adaptation lessons on copies of the same Domain-1 LiteTCN. Ablations are CARE with one lesson-rule removed. The oracle is a labelled upper bound, not a competitor we claim to beat in the product.”

1. Problem (deliverable: understand Domain 1 vs Domain 2)

DONE Say it in baby words:

Domain 1 = the old place, we have the answer sheet (past and future). We train a model: look at last week, guess tomorrow.
Domain 2 = the new place. We can see the past. We are not allowed to use the future while we train or while we pick the model. That is unsupervised domain adaptation for forecasting.

Course examples: hills → plains weather, IT stock → pharma stock. We did both, plus two electricity transformers.

Shimla mean temp ≈ 11.4 °C, Delhi ≈ 24.6 °C. The two places barely overlap. Copying the old model is not enough.

2. Background (deliverable: study existing methods)

DONE Four forecasting ideas we used: DLinear (a straight line can be strong), TCN (cheap long memory), PatchTST (strong but heavy, later), RevIN (remove this window’s own average so “Delhi is hotter” is less of a problem).

Theory in one line (Ben-David): new-place error ≤ old-place error + how different the two places look inside the model + a leftover we cannot measure without answers. So we keep old skill, make the insides look alike (alignment), and do not smash useful features.

Time-series papers: CoDATS (adversary), SASA (variable links), DAF (forecasting but needs some labels), CLUDA (compare similar windows), RAINCOAT (frequency). Gap we picked: forecasting + no labels + small model.

5. Lightweight Domain-1 model (deliverable: small forecaster)

DONE Name: LiteTCN-Forecaster. File: src/model.py. Weights: models/climate/source_model.pt (same for energy, finance).

A window goes in → RevIN takes off its average → 3 convolution layers squeeze it to 128 numbers (the summary z) → a linear head guesses the future → we put the average back. A second straight-line path (DLinear) sits beside it so it stays stable. About 380,000 weights. One epoch < 1 minute on a laptop.

The summary z is later where we compare Shimla vs Delhi. That is why the model is built for adaptation, not only for forecasting.

How training works (if they ask): Domain 1 is trained 20 epochs with AdamW, learning rate 0.001, batch 256. We keep the snapshot with the best Domain-1 validation error. Adaptation is 10 epochs, smaller learning rate 0.0003, 256 Domain-1 windows + 256 Domain-2 windows each step. v1 keeps the last epoch. v2 picks the snapshot with the smallest (Domain-1 val error + how different the two summaries still are) — still no Domain-2 answers.

6. Adaptation strategy (deliverable: how Domain 2 is taught)

DONE (v1 full) v2 one seed

Name: CARE-DA = Correlation-Aligned, Reconstruction-regularised, Elastic-anchored Domain Adaptation.

One training step: Shimla batch with answers + Delhi batch without answers, same network.
1. Keep Shimla forecasts good.
2. Make the two summaries look alike (MMD + CORAL).
3. Do a no-answers homework on Delhi.
4. Stay close to the starting weights (spring / anchor) so we do not forget Domain 1.

v1 homework = hide 30% of Delhi’s past and rebuild it. We ran this on 3 seeds. It hurt.

v2 homework = a forecast-like task on Delhi (still no answers) + extra alignment. The app’s adapted_model.pt is v2, seed 0 only. v1 is kept as adapted_model_v1.pt so we can overlay both.

This is the Domain-2 deliverable in a picture: grey = input, black = real future, orange dashed = Domain-1 model reused, blue = adapted model.

8. Loss function (deliverable: design the adaptation loss)

DONE This is a required designed object, not just “we trained something”.

v1 (the designed loss we evaluated fully):

L = forecast_error(Shimla) + MMD(z_s, z_t) + CORAL(z_s, z_t) + mask_rebuild(Delhi) + 0.01 × distance_to_old_weights

  • MMD = a ruler that asks “are these two clouds of points the same?”
  • CORAL = match how variables move together.
  • Same weights on all 3 datasets on purpose (fair vs baselines).

Ablation: turn off alignment → everything gets worse. Turn off mask-rebuild → everything gets better. So the designed full v1 is honest, but not the best row.

Turning up the rebuild-input weight makes error worse on every dataset. That is a real negative result, and it is why v2 exists.

9. Metrics (deliverable: goodness of forecast)

DONE The brief asks for performance / goodness-of-forecast metrics. We show them in the app and in the tables.

  • MSE / MAE — average miss, after rescaling so every variable counts.
  • MASE — are we better than “repeat yesterday / last season”? Below 1 = yes.
  • Key RMSE — real units a user understands (°C temperature, % range, °C oil).

13. If the TA quizzes you

What is a seed? Why 3? A seed is the starting number for randomness. We rerun the same recipe three times (0, 1, 2) and report the average so one lucky run cannot fool us. v2 has only seed 0 so far.
How many models did you try? One network (LiteTCN). Eleven recipes: seasonal naive, source-only, DANN, CORAL, MMD, CARE v1, three ablations, CARE v2, plus two labelled references (oracle, target-only). 3 datasets × 3 seeds for the first campaign.
What worked best? Among fair 3-seed no-label methods: MMD on climate (−7.9%), CARE without SSL on energy (−6.4%) and finance (−0.6%). Full designed CARE v1 is good but not best. v2 looks better on 1 seed.
What failed? DANN, CORAL-alone, and CARE with alignment removed. Also the rebuild-input homework inside v1.
Did you use Domain-2 labels? No. Not for training, not for picking the checkpoint. v2 picks min(source-val MSE + feature MMD).
Why three datasets? Brief asks ≥ 3. Hills/plains and IT/pharma are the brief’s own examples. ETT is a standard public pair so we are not only on our weather data.
Why small model? Brief asks lightweight. Adaptation is repeated per new place. Laptop CPU.
What is MMD / CORAL / DANN? MMD = match the whole cloud of summaries. CORAL = match correlations. DANN = a guesser tries to tell which domain a summary came from; we try to fool it. DANN failed for us.
Is your designed method the best? Honest: full CARE-DA v1 is not the best row. Alignment is. The extra rebuild task hurt. v2 is the fix, one seed only.
Why is finance weak? Even the oracle that uses pharma answers only gains 2.4%. Next-day returns are noisy. We report it as a real negative result, not a hidden failure.
Did Domain 1 get worse? No. CARE-DA / MMD slightly improve Domain 1. Using Domain-2 answers instead makes Domain 1 about 14% worse on climate.
Where are the models? models/<dataset>/source_model.pt (Domain 1) and adapted_model.pt (Domain 2, v2). v1 kept as adapted_model_v1.pt.
